The Truth About 'Topping Off' Freon: Why Leak Detection Comes First

A central air conditioner is a sealed closed-loop refrigeration circuit. It does not consume or 'burn' refrigerant like an automobile burns fuel or motor oil. If your system is low on R-410A (or legacy R-22), it has a physical leak. Simply adding Freon without locating and repairing the leak is temporary, expensive, and non-compliant with EPA Clean Air guidelines.

Advanced Electronic Leak Detection in Poway Homes

Technicians utilize three rigorous leak detection methods: (1) Ultrasonic & Heated Diode Electronic Sniffers: Capable of detecting micro-leaks down to 0.1 ounces per year along copper line sets, service valves, and aluminum U-bends. (2) Nitrogen Decay Testing: Pressurizing the isolated line set with dry nitrogen up to 250 PSI to identify pressure drop over time. (3) UV Fluorescent Dye: Adding non-harmful dye visible under ultraviolet lamps to isolate micro-fractures in coils.

R-410A Phase-Down & Legacy R-22 Realities

The EPA completely banned production and importation of R-22 in 2020. If your home has an older R-22 unit installed before 2010 that develops a major coil leak, virgin R-22 supplies are scarce and costly. Dispatched technicians evaluate whether retrofitting with approved replacement blends or upgrading to modern ultra-efficient systems is the best financial choice.
