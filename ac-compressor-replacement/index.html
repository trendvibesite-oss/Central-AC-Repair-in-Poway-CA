The Heart of Your Central AC System

The compressor is the mechanical heart of your air conditioning system. Located inside the outdoor condensing unit, it compresses low-pressure, cool refrigerant gas into high-pressure, superheated vapor, driving the continuous heat exchange cycle. In Poway's demanding climate, where summer peak temperatures often hover near triple digits for consecutive days, compressors endure intense mechanical and thermal punishment.

Winding Diagnostics: Ground Shorts vs. Mechanical Seizures

Dispatched technicians never guess on compressor failures. They perform rigorous electrical diagnostics using digital multimeters and high-voltage insulation megohmmeters (Meggers). By measuring resistance between the Common (C), Start (S), and Run (R) terminals, technicians verify winding continuity (C-to-R + C-to-S must equal R-to-S) and ensure resistance to copper ground exceeds 20 Megohms. If a short to ground or locked rotor is detected, replacement options are presented.

Replace Compressor vs. Replace Condenser: An Honest Comparison

Replacing an out-of-warranty hermetic compressor requires recovering refrigerant, unbrazing copper suction and discharge lines with oxy-acetylene torches, flushing lines, brazing in a new OEM scroll, installing dual liquid line filter-driers, pulling a deep vacuum below 500 microns, and charging with virgin R-410A. If your AC system is over 10-12 years old, technicians will honestly evaluate whether investing in a compressor rebuild or upgrading to a high-efficiency 16+ SEER2 condensing unit offers better long-term financial value.
